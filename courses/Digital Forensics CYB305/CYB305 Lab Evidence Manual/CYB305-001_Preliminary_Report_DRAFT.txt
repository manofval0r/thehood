4. Findings and limits
Observed facts. Two portal edits fall inside the approved window. The banner edit matches E07 and is logged by the portal and the web server. The beneficiary edit is logged by both systems under the same ticket reference, but E07 places that field out of scope and no separate approval appears in the export. On the evidence supplied, the beneficiary edit is recorded and unauthorised.
Conclusion. The records show an approved banner change by it.support and an unapproved beneficiary change by bursary.ops, both inside the 09:00 to 09:30 window. They do not show who was at the keyboard, or whether money moved.
Alternative explanation. The approval may exist outside this export. The account may have been misused after two failed logins, or an administrator may have changed the wrong field. No transaction ledger was supplied, so loss and diversion remain unproven.
Additional evidence needed. The separate authorisation for ALT-909, the full change history with signatures, MFA and session binding logs, proxy and NAT logs for 203.0.113.45, images of the portal and mail hosts, the transaction ledger, and statements from bursary.ops and the change coordinator.

5. Evidence handling and recommendations
Custody issue. E09 CUST-002 records a transfer from ICT Triage to the Forensic Desk at 10:19 WAT with no receiving acknowledgement. CUST-001 shows RT and CUST-003 shows FS. The gap sits at the handover point, so continuity of the evidence cannot be shown for that step. Each transfer should record the receiver name, exact time, hash check and signature.
Immediate preservation. Freeze the portal configuration, suspend routine admin access, preserve authentication, web, mail and proxy logs, secure the transaction ledger, and image the portal and mail hosts. Hash each image at collection.
Forensic readiness. Central logs with a trusted time source, MFA enforced with the result recorded, system rules that block out of scope edits, and automatic hash manifests at intake.
Legal and ethical safeguard (Nigeria). The Evidence Act 2011 as amended governs the reliability of electronic records. The Cybercrimes Act 2015 as amended covers unauthorised access and preservation duties. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 limits use of personal data to a lawful basis. A matching hash supports integrity. It says nothing about authorship, admissibility or lawful collection.
